Oslo Bysykkel · first model

Can one night truck keep Oslo's bikes where riders need them?

Oslo's city bikes roll downhill. By evening the stations in the hills are empty and the centre is full. Here one truck works from 01:00 to 06:00 and carries bikes back. We replay four weeks of real trips three times and count every rider who was let down: no bike to take, or no free dock to park it.

How the optimized truck decides

From eleven weeks of earlier trips we learn one curve per station: if this station starts the day with this many bikes, how many riders will it let down? A hill station wants to start full, because riders take bikes all morning. A station in the centre wants to start nearly empty, because riders arrive all day and need a free dock.

Each night the model looks at where the bikes stand at 01:00 and chooses the route and the number of bikes to load or unload at each stop, so that the sum of all the curves is as low as possible. It must fit the shift, never carry more than the truck holds, and end the night empty.

The rule of thumb is what a dispatcher might do without a model: drive to the fullest station, load until it is half full, drive to the emptiest station, unload until it is half full, and repeat.

What we assumed

  • The fleet is 3,000 bikes, the operator's own figure. Real evening inventories are not published, so every run starts with all stations about half full.
  • The truck holds 20 bikes and works five hours. A stop costs 3 minutes plus half a minute per bike. It drives at 25 km/h over 1.4 times the straight-line distance, from an assumed depot at Økern. The map draws straight lines between stops.
  • A rider who finds no bike gives up. A rider who finds no free dock rides on to the nearest station with room. Both count as let down.
  • Nobody moves bikes during the day. The real operator does, which is why the run with no truck looks worse than real life.
  • The planner only sees trips from 1 June to 16 August. It never sees the trips of the day it plans for.
  • Recorded trips understate demand, because an empty station records no departures.
