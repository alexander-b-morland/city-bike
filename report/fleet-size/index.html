How many night trucks does Oslo need?

One truck could not keep up. Here several trucks work the same night, driving real Oslo streets from the operator's base at Kværnerveien. We replay the same four weeks for each fleet size and count the riders let down: no bike to take, or no free dock to park it.

How Oslo Bysykkel does it in real life

  • The base. The operator, Urban Infrastructure Partner, has its head office, warehouse and operations centre at Kværnerveien 5, east of the centre. Our trucks start and end there. Source
  • The system. About 3,000 bikes, both ordinary and electric, at around 260 stations inside Ring 3. Source
  • The crews. Teams of mechanics and drivers of specially adapted trucks. In summer much of the repair work is done at the stations. In 2018 the operator wrote that it had vehicles out around the clock, every day. Source
  • Planning. Researchers at NTNU built an optimization model with the operator in which a driver gets a new plan at every station. In their tests it cut problems by 41% against no rebalancing and by 24% against the operator's practice at the time. Source
  • Not published. We found no public figure for how many trucks the operator runs or how many bikes a truck carries. The 20 bikes per truck used here is our assumption.

What we assumed

  • Trucks work only at night, from 01:00 to 06:00, and nobody moves bikes during the day. The real operator works around the clock, so real life looks better than every line on this page.
  • Each truck holds 20 bikes. A stop costs 3 minutes plus half a minute per bike. Trucks drive at 70% of the speed limit along OpenStreetMap streets, respecting one-way streets.
  • Trucks are planned one at a time: each gets the best plan given what the trucks before it will do. On test nights this came within 1% of planning all trucks together.
  • The fleet is 3,000 bikes and every run starts with all stations about half full on 17 August 2026.
  • A rider who finds no bike gives up. A rider who finds no free dock rides on to the nearest station with room. Both count as let down.
  • The planner learns from trips between 1 June and 16 August and never sees the day it plans for.
  • The costs are ours, not the operator's. A truck night of 3,000 kr assumes about 5.5 paid hours at 450 kr including employer costs and night supplement, plus about 500 kr for the van.
