When should the trucks work?

Night trucks set the city up for the morning. By the afternoon the bikes have moved again, and no number of night trucks can fix that. Here some of the trucks work at midday instead, from 10:00 to 15:00, between the two rush hours.

Choosing the shift times

A truck on the road Rush hour

How the shifts were chosen. A shift may start at 01, 04, 07, 10, 13, 16 or 19 o'clock, and at most five trucks are on the road at once. Starting from no trucks, every possible extra shift was tried, and the one that lowered the total cost a day most was kept, until no extra shift lowered it. The search compared schedules on two earlier weeks, 3 to 16 August, so that the four weeks above are a fair test.

How the two shifts are planned

Each shift gets its own plan, made when it starts, from where the bikes are at that moment. What changes is how far ahead each plan looks.

  • The night shift plans for the whole day, as if no other trucks followed. It sets up the morning rush and does what it can for the afternoon.
  • The midday shift plans for the rest of the day. It starts from where the morning left the bikes, and its curves count only the riders a station would let down after 10:00.
  • Midday driving is slower. We add 30% to every driving time for traffic.

What to keep in mind

  • The midday plan treats every delivery as if it happened at 10:00, although the truck arrives up to five hours later, while riders keep moving bikes. A plan that is renewed at every stop, as the real operator's is, would do better than what is shown here.
  • Trucks, shifts, bikes and riders are otherwise as on the fleet-size page: 20 bikes per truck, five-hour shifts, 3,000 bikes, and every run starts on 17 August with all stations about half full.
  • The solver is stopped after 10 seconds per truck, so better plans may exist.
